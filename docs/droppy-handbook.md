## What Droppy is

 A native macOS app that turns the top of your screen into a working surface: a file tray, a clipboard history, media controls, live status, and a store of optional tools called Droplets.

 On a MacBook with a notch, Droppy lives in the notch and grows out of it. On an iMac, a Mac mini or any external display, the same surface appears as a floating Dynamic Island pill instead. Nothing is missing on a Mac without a notch, and you choose which of the two shapes each display uses.

 Everything runs locally. Files you drop, text you copy and screens you capture stay on your Mac unless you deliberately share them. The only two features that talk to a server are Droppy Cloud, which uploads what you choose, and Droppy Sync, which carries end to end encrypted records to your iPhone.

## The four things it does

 A tray for filesDrag anything to the top of the screen, or shake to bring a floating tray to your cursor, and it waits there until you drag it back out, anywhere else.

 A clipboard that remembersEvery copy is kept, searchable and ready to paste again, with the app it came from.

 Live status, not pop-upsVolume, brightness, battery, timers, calls, recordings and downloads all report from one place.

 40 optional DropletsFree extensions the size of apps you would otherwise buy. They only run when you switch them on.

## What you need

 RequiresmacOS 14 Sonoma or later. Apple Silicon and Intel, notched and non-notch Macs.

## Install and activate

 Download, drag, launch, activate. Four steps, and the trial starts without a license key.

- Download DroppyGet the `.dmg` from getdroppy.app. The download is signed and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens it without a warning.

- Drag Droppy to ApplicationsOpen the disk image and drag the app into the Applications folder shown beside it. Droppy has to live in Applications: some features refuse to run from the disk image or the Downloads folder, and Droppy tells you if it finds itself in the wrong place.

- Launch itDroppy appears at the top of your screen and in the menu bar. Onboarding walks you through the permissions it needs and lets you pick your first Droplets.

- Activate, or start the trialEnter your license key under Settings, License. Without a key, the 3-day trial runs everything, so you can try the whole app first.

## Updating

 Droppy updates itself, and an update never touches your data. Settings, clipboard history, file tray contents, Droplet configuration and your license all carry across, including a jump between release channels.

## First run and permissions

 macOS asks before an app may watch the screen or read input. Droppy asks for the minimum it needs to draw its surface, then asks again only when a Droplet you turn on genuinely needs more.

## The two that matter on day one

 AccessibilityLets Droppy place its overlay and receive the global shortcuts you record.

 Screen RecordingLets Droppy read the pixels behind its own surface, which is what makes the notch blend into your wallpaper, and what capture, OCR and meeting features rely on.

 Everything else is optional and asked for in context. The full list, with what each one unlocks, is on Permissions.

## If a prompt never appeared

 macOS shows each privacy prompt once. If you dismissed one, open Settings, Permissions in Droppy and use that row's button: it either asks macOS directly or jumps straight to the right pane in System Settings. Full Disk Access needs Droppy to relaunch after you grant it, and the row says so.

## The menu bar and Settings

 Droppy has no main window. Its menu bar icon and its Settings window are where you configure it, and both are reachable from the surface itself.

## The menu bar icon

 The icon opens Droppy's own menu: your active Droppy Cloud shares with a delete button for each, Voice Transcribe's Quick Record, Stay Awake, the floating file tray, Settings, and the update check.

 You can hide the icon entirely under Settings, General, and bring it back by right-clicking the notch silhouette where it used to sit.

## Opening Settings

 Right-click the notch or the island to open Settings from anywhere. What that right-click does is itself a setting, under Settings, Accessibility: open Settings, or hide the surface until you right-click the same spot again.

 Settings has a search field of its own. It searches every setting on every page as well as the Droplet catalogue, so if you know roughly what a thing is called you never have to remember which tab it is on.

## Dock icon and launch at login

 Droppy runs without a Dock icon by default, which also keeps it out of ⌘Tab. Turn the Dock icon on under Settings, General, Startup and visibility if you would rather have it. The same card holds Launch at login, which needs Droppy allowed under System Settings, General, Login Items.

## The notch and the island

 One surface, two shapes. Droppy either grows out of the hardware notch or floats as a Dynamic Island pill, and you choose which per display. It opens when you hover or drag, and holds your widgets.

## How you interact with it

- Hover opens the notch. Whether hovering is enough on your main Mac and on external displays is set under Auto-expand in Settings, General, Expanded notch.
 
- Drag onto it and it opens to take the drop, wherever the drag started.
 
- Click runs whatever you set under On notch click in Settings, Media, Gestures.
 
- Two-finger swipe moves between the notch's pages and Now Playing, and scrubs inside the media controls. Turn gestures on or off under Settings, General, Expanded notch, Gestures.
 
- Right-click opens Settings, or hides the surface, depending on what you set under Settings, Accessibility.

## Pages and navigation

 The open notch has three pages: Home, Tray and Widgets. Tray holds the file tray when it appears in the notch. The page buttons sit below the notch and can be hidden with Show navigation, in which case you move between pages with a two-finger swipe instead. The tray button can also be set to appear only while files are actually waiting.

## Home widgets

 Any Droplet with a notch widget can be placed on the Home page, alone or beside another one. Widgets available today cover files, weather, media, notifications, Stay Awake, Termi-Notch, Pomodoro, camera, Voice Transcribe, Calendar, Notes, Obsidian, Thaw, Agents, Mechey, clipboard, System Stats, Text Capture and Audio Control.

 Widgets that are mostly a text field, meaning Notes, Obsidian and Termi-Notch, take keyboard focus when you open them on their own, so the cursor is already in the field. Widgets that are not text fields never steal focus.

 Arrange them under Settings, General, Widgets.

 Home widgetsShows your current layout. Its button opens the notch itself so you can add, remove or rearrange up to four widgets.

 Show widgets pageAdds the Widgets button below the notch and its page of widget icons.

 Widget iconsRearranges the icons on the Widgets page.

 FavoritesPins up to four widgets, apps or shortcuts beside the navigation bar for one-tap access.

 Animation speedMakes the notch and island open and close faster or slower.

## Shape, size and appearance

 Under Settings, Theming you pick the surface style for notched displays and for notchless displays independently, so a MacBook can use the notch while the monitor beside it uses the island. Width and height for both shapes are adjustable there too, along with island position.

 Highlight colorTints Droppy's accents. Separate colors are available for the media player text, the lock screen widget text, and the volume and brightness slider fills.

 Window tintGives Droppy's windows an opaque backing instead of glass, so text stays legible on a dark wallpaper.

 Show on external displays, Per-display visibility, Hide physical notch and Hide on fullscreen are under Settings, General, Visibility.

 Hide physical notchDraws a black bar over the hardware notch so the whole strip reads as one shape.

 Per-display visibilityChoose exactly which connected displays show the notch or island.

 Show on external displaysNever keeps Droppy on the built-in screen only, When active shows it on a monitor while something is on it, and Always keeps it there at rest too.

 Hide on fullscreenHide the notch or island while an app is fullscreen. To hide only Now Playing, use Hide media in fullscreen under Settings, Media instead.

## The file tray

 Where the files you are moving wait. It sits in the notch, floats beside your cursor, or both, and holds what you drop until you drag it out somewhere else.

 Drag any file, folder, image or selection to the top of the screen, or shake while dragging. The file tray opens to take it and holds it until you drag it out somewhere else, drop it on a Quick Action, or clear it. Items can be pinned so they survive automatic clean-up.

## Where it appears

 Settings, File tray opens with one question: where does the file tray appear?

 In the notchThe tray is a page of the open notch. Drag a file to the top of the screen and the notch opens to take it.

 FloatingThe tray comes to your cursor instead, as the floating file tray.

 BothThe default. The notch keeps its tray and the floating file tray comes when you call it, so you use whichever is closer.

 The switch in the page's toolbar turns the file tray off entirely. The notch still opens for widgets and media.

## In the notch

 Open tray after dropSwitches to the Tray page after a successful drop, so what you just added is visible.

 File retentionRemoves unpinned files from the notch's tray after a time you choose.

 How the notch opens, its size and its pages are under Settings, General, Expanded notch. See The notch and the island.

 Instant appear and its delay, Shake sensitivity, Auto-hide and its delay, and Multiple floating trays. Each one is explained on The floating file tray.

## Both trays

 Shown while the file tray appears in both places.

 Sync between traysKeeps files and removals in sync between the notch's tray and the floating file tray.

 These two are under Settings, General, File handling.

## The floating file tray

 A file tray that comes to your cursor. Start dragging, shake, and it flies in to hold what you are carrying until you decide where it goes.

 The floating file tray exists for the drag you have not finished planning: gather files from three folders, then drop the whole pile in one move. It follows your pointer instead of living at the top of the screen, so it works the same on every display.

 It is one of the places the file tray can appear: Floating on its own, or Both, which is the default. Its options are in the Floating file tray section of Settings, File tray.

## How to reveal it

- Shake while dragging. Shake sensitivity runs from low to very high.
 
- Instant appear brings it in the moment a drag starts, with no shake at all. Its delay sets how long a drag runs before it appears.
 
- A drag shortcut reveals it on a key you hold while dragging. Setting one turns shake and instant appear off, because the key is now the deliberate signal. Record it on the Shortcuts page.

## Auto-hide

 With Auto-hide on, the floating file tray puts itself away once the pointer leaves it, after a delay you choose. Together with the appear delay, that lets it be eager or patient depending on how you work.

## One or multiple floating trays

 By default there is only ever one floating file tray. Turn on Multiple floating trays and shaking while one is already open brings another, so you can sort a pile into several destinations in a single pass.

 The Tray Switcher shows every open floating file tray at once and moves between them. Give it a shortcut on the Shortcuts page.

 Floating file trays carry the same Quick Action tiles as the notch, so a gathered pile can go to AirDrop, a cloud link or Mail without being dropped anywhere first. With Both, Sync between trays keeps the floating file tray and the notch's tray holding the same files.

## Clipboard

 Every copy you make, kept and searchable, with the app it came from. Open it from anywhere with a shortcut and paste something from three days ago.

 History holds text, rich text, links, images, colors and file references. Entries can be pinned, favorited and tagged, and text recognized by Text Capture is indexed alongside the image it came from, so searching for a word in a screenshot finds the screenshot.

## Using it

- Open history with the shortcut you record on the Shortcuts page. It works from any app.
 
- Search by typing. With auto-focus search on, your first keystroke opens the search field already carrying that character; with it off, nothing is focused at open, so the arrow keys navigate and Esc closes in one press.
 
- Type filters narrow the list to one kind of entry from a rail beside it.
 
- Copy + favorite is a second shortcut that copies an entry and stars it in one press.
 
- Item shortcuts act on the selected entry, and only work while the clipboard window is open and focused.

## Options

 Clipboard layoutNew is the modern bottom strip; Legacy is the previous layout, kept for people who preferred it.

 Reject duplicatesSkips re-copies of text, URLs or colors already in history instead of bumping them to the top.

 Titles and source appShow each entry's title on a blurred pill, and the icon of the app it came from beside it.

 TagsLabel entries so a set stays together and stays findable.

 Clear all except pinnedDeletes every entry except the pinned, favorited and tagged ones.

 Clear history on quitWipes history and removes it from disk when Droppy quits.

## Import history

 Open Import history from the clipboard window or Settings, Clipboard, History. Imports add to your existing history and skip duplicate text. Your history limit and retention period still apply; increase them before importing if you want to keep more entries.

- Maccy: click Import to read its local history. Text, links, pins and rich-text formatting are supported.
 
- Raycast v2: run Export Settings & Data in Raycast with Clipboard History included, choose the resulting `.rayconfig` file in Droppy, and enter the export passphrase. Text and links are supported. Raycast pins and formatting are not imported.

 Image-only, file-only and grouped entries are skipped and counted in the result. The source app's history is never changed. Imported entries participate in Droppy Sync when clipboard sync is enabled.

 Raycast export decryption uses CryptoSwift. This product includes software developed by the "Marcin Krzyzanowski" (http://krzyzanowskim.com/).

## Privacy

 Clipboard history never leaves your Mac unless you turn on Droppy Sync, and then it is sealed on the device before it is sent. Droppy honours the system flag apps use to mark a copy as concealed, so password managers do not end up in your history.

## Live Activities

 The status side of Droppy. Instead of pop-ups scattered across the screen, everything reports from the notch or the island, and more than one thing can report at once.

 System and Droplet activities have their switches under Settings, Live Activities, so you can keep the ones you care about and turn the rest off. The Now Playing switch is under Settings, Media, Notch.

## System

- Volume, brightness and keyboard brightness replace the macOS pop-ups. Each can be switched independently, and an optional soft tick plays on key presses.
 
- Battery and charging, with low-battery alerts and one-tap Low Power Mode.
 
- AirPods and headphones connecting, with their battery level.
 
- Caps Lock on and off, and Focus mode changes.
 
- External drives connecting, with a safe eject button.
 
- Network: joining a network, hotspot battery, live VPN status with a session timer, and an optional offline warning.
 
- Recording and privacy indicators: screen recording controls with a video preview, and the microphone and screen indicators.
 
- Droppy updates when a new version is available.

## Media

 The media widget shows artwork, title, artist and a scrubber, with the full player and your Playing Next queue one swipe away. It also carries the native AirPlay and audio output picker. These options are under Settings, Media.

 The floating player puts playback controls in the menu bar and on your desktop, with three sizes, motion artwork, Playing Next and lyrics.

- Live audio visualizer bounces the bars to what is actually playing. macOS asks once for System Audio Recording; Droppy reads levels in the moment and never records.
 
- Motion artwork plays Apple Music and Spotify animated covers when the song has one, and pauses with playback to stay light on CPU.
 
- Notch track title briefly shows track info under the physical notch on song change.
 
- Track swipe changes songs with a swipe, in the direction you prefer.
 
- Hide Incognito media keeps anything playing in a private browsing window out of the notch.
 
- Default music app decides which player the controls target when more than one could answer.

## From Droplets

 Droplets report here too, once switched on: Pomodoro's focus and break timers, Stay Awake, Meetings with a call timer and live audio, Notifications, Agents showing Claude, Codex and Cursor status, Termi-Notch, the Calendar event progress ring, and the file tray's own drop confirmation.

## More than one at a time

 Droppy gives the primary activity the main seat and pills the others beside it, so a timer keeps ticking while a track changes and a download finishes.

## Floating player

 Your music, wherever you want it. One player moves between a small capsule, everyday controls and a large animated cover, with Playing Next and lyrics inside the same surface.

 Watch the floating player in motion, including its size changes, song artwork, queue and lyrics.

## Open it and make it float

- Open Settings, Media, Floating player and turn on Player in the menu bar. Its menu bar item shows the current artwork and waveform while music is playing.
 
- Click the menu bar item to open the player. Click outside to close the drop-down, or shrink it past its smallest size with a vertical gesture.
 
- Drag the open player away from the menu bar to keep it on your desktop. You can also turn on Float over the desktop in Settings, or right-click the menu bar item and choose Show Floating Player.
 
- Drag the floating player wherever it belongs. Choose Above all apps to keep it in reach over your windows, or Pin to desktop to place it behind them.

## Three sizes, one continuous motion

 MiniA small capsule with artwork, the song title and play/pause.

 PlayerArtwork, title and artist, a playback timeline and transport controls. Lyrics and Playing Next open in a drawer.

 ExpandedA large cover with playback controls, volume and audio outputs. Open Playing Next or lyrics without leaving the player.

- Click the surface outside the artwork and controls to grow to the next size. Clicking the expanded surface closes an open page first, then returns to Player. Clicking the artwork opens the playing app.
 
- Move two fingers vertically over the player to expand or collapse it. The edge follows your fingers and settles when you release. Near the bottom of the screen it grows upward to keep the controls within reach.
 
- Use the mouse wheel to step between sizes. You can also choose Mini, Player or Expanded under Settings, Media, Floating player, Size.
 
- Swipe horizontally to go to the previous or next track. The player follows the same track-swipe direction as the notch player.
 
- Drag the timeline to seek, or the volume slider to change the output level. Scrolling a queue or output list scrolls that list instead of resizing the player.

## Motion artwork

 Turn on Motion artwork under Settings, Media, Floating player. Animated covers keep moving as the player changes size, and fill the expanded player when the song has a matching motion cover.

 Apple Music uses animated artwork from its catalog. Spotify uses Canvas when available, or matching Apple Music motion artwork when a Canvas is unavailable. Availability depends on the song and the source: other apps, missing artwork and unavailable matches keep the regular still cover. Not every song has motion art.

 Reduce Motion keeps the artwork still. The Motion artwork switch also controls the floating player used on the lock screen.

## Playing Next and lyrics

 Playing NextOpen the queue for Apple Music or YouTube Music in Kaset. Browse the upcoming tracks and choose what plays next. Spotify and other sources do not offer a queue in this player.

 LyricsInstall the Lyrics Droplet and open the lyrics button in Expanded, or the L badge beside a track title when timed lyrics are available. Lines follow playback. The page tells you when the song has no synced lyrics.

 Keep the coverWith motion artwork in Expanded, lyrics sit over the dimmed moving cover. Playing Next and audio outputs open their own page inside the player.

 Favorite, shuffle and repeat controls depend on the playing app. Apple Music also offers Add to Playlist. The output button lets you choose an available audio device and adjust its volume.

## Appearance and availability

 The player is available on macOS 14 Sonoma and later, on Macs with or without a notch. Detecting playback from native music apps requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later. Native Liquid Glass and the optional Dynamic Glass finish require macOS 26 or later. Dynamic Glass fades from black at the top into glass below; older systems use a dark surface.

 Turn on Show when nothing is playing to keep the player available between songs and sessions. The floating player and the notch's Now Playing controls have separate switches on the Media settings page.

 The lock screen uses the same player in its Now Playing card, with controls suited to the locked screen.

## Droppy Cloud

 Drop files, get a private link, send it anywhere. The recipient opens it in a browser. No account on either end.

 Drop files into the file tray and pick the cloud Quick Action, or use the Droppy Cloud Droplet. Droppy uploads them and copies a `getdroppy.app/cloud/<slug>` link to your clipboard. Every active share is listed in the menu bar with its own delete button.

## Limits

 Per file200 MB maximum.

 Per share1 GB total, and at most 25 files.

 Per installation1 GB of active shared data at any one time. Delete an active share or wait for one to expire to free room.

 Per network address3 GB of active shared data.

 Expiry3 days after upload, automatically. You can delete a share yourself at any time before that, and the files go for good.

 StorageFile contents live on Cloudflare R2. The slug, filename, type, size, upload time and expiry time live in our database.

## Not end to end encrypted

 Uploads and downloads use HTTPS, and files at rest are encrypted server-side by Cloudflare, but Droppy Cloud is not end to end encrypted. If you need confidential transfer, encrypt the files yourself first or use a tool built for that.

Droppy Sync, which carries your library to your iPhone, is a separate path and is end to end encrypted.

 Full detail, including exactly what is stored per share, is on the Droppy Cloud page.

## MCP

 AI apps such as Claude, Claude Code, Cursor and Codex can use Droppy through the Model Context Protocol, only with your permission.

 MCP is the open standard AI apps use to reach the tools on your Mac. Droppy speaks it, so you can ask Claude to find something you copied or start a Pomodoro, and it happens in Droppy, inside the limits you set.

## What AI apps can do

- Search your clipboard history.
 
- Find files on the file tray, in floating file trays, in Droppy Cloud, and among your recordings and screenshots.
 
- Read your calendar, to-dos and notes, and add to them.
 
- Start a timer, a Pomodoro or Stay Awake (High Alert).
 
- Show an alert in the notch.
 
- Play, pause and skip what is playing, and set the volume.
 
- Read and change Droppy's settings.
 
- Find Droplets in the Store, install them, and switch them on or off.
 
- Convert and compress files with Transform.
 
- Take screenshots and recognize the text in them.
 
- Search and run Thunderstorm commands.

 New Droppy features and the Droplets you install show up in the AI app on their own after an update. There is nothing to reconfigure.

## Turn it on

 Open Settings, MCP and turn on Allow AI apps to use Droppy. It is off until you do, and turning it off again disconnects every app at once.

 Then pick what AI apps may do. Each permission is its own switch, and every one is off until you turn it on. An app only sees the tools that belong to the permissions you allowed.

## Connect an app

 The quickest way is the Connect button under Settings, MCP, Connect an app, for Claude, Claude Code, Cursor and Codex. Droppy names the file it is about to change and waits for you to confirm. It adds only its own entry, leaves your other servers alone, and keeps the old file next to it as a backup. Restart the app afterwards so it picks Droppy up.

 Connect needs Droppy in your Applications folder, because the path it writes has to survive a restart. If Droppy is not running when an app needs it, the app starts it.

 To set it up by hand, every app gets the same thing: the small helper inside Droppy, `/Applications/Droppy.app/Contents/Helpers/droppy-mcp`. If your copy of Droppy lives somewhere else, use its path instead.

### Claude

 Add this to `~/Library/Application Support/Claude/claude_desktop_config.json`, next to any servers already in it, then quit and reopen Claude.

 `{
 "mcpServers": {
 "droppy": {
 "command": "/Applications/Droppy.app/Contents/Helpers/droppy-mcp",
 "args": []
 }
 }
}`

### Claude Code

 Run this once in Terminal. `--scope user` makes Droppy available in every project.

 `claude mcp add --scope user droppy -- /Applications/Droppy.app/Contents/Helpers/droppy-mcp`

### Cursor

 Add this to `~/.cursor/mcp.json`, then restart Cursor.

 `{
 "mcpServers": {
 "droppy": {
 "type": "stdio",
 "command": "/Applications/Droppy.app/Contents/Helpers/droppy-mcp",
 "args": []
 }
 }
}`

### Codex

 Run this once in Terminal:

 `codex mcp add droppy -- /Applications/Droppy.app/Contents/Helpers/droppy-mcp`

 Or add the table yourself to `~/.codex/config.toml`. It is what Connect writes, and its two timeouts give Codex time to start Droppy and give you time to answer Droppy's questions, so add them after `codex mcp add` too.

 `[mcp_servers.droppy]
command = "/Applications/Droppy.app/Contents/Helpers/droppy-mcp"
args = []
startup_timeout_sec = 30
tool_timeout_sec = 180`

### Any other app

## You stay in control

- You approve each app. The first time an app connects, Droppy asks whether to allow it. Until you do, all it can see is which permissions are on. If the app is a script runner such as Node or Python, Droppy says so: allowing it allows every script it runs.
 
- Droppy asks before anything sensitive. Screenshots, recordings, reading a file's contents, uploads, installs, script commands and anything that deletes always ask in Droppy first, unless you choose Always allow for that app and that action. No answer within two minutes counts as no.
 
- You see every call. Settings, MCP, Activity lists what each app did and how it went. It never records what was sent or what came back.
 
- You can take it back. Revoke one app under Connected apps, or all of them with Revoke all. Anything you always allowed can be reset under Always allowed.

 Never shared with any app: your license, your account, payment details, passwords and the Keychain, and clipboard items your password manager marked as concealed.

Nothing listens on the network. The AI app talks to Droppy through a private connection on your Mac, and Droppy recognizes the app by its code signature, not by the name it gives itself.

 One thing to keep in mind: an AI app does what its model decides, and a model can be steered by a web page or a file it reads. Turn on only the permissions you use, and read Droppy's question before you press Allow.

## Pairing and sync

 Droppy for iPhone is a companion to Droppy on the Mac. Scan one code and your clipboard, files, notes, OCR grabs and cloud links are on your phone, end to end encrypted.

 The app is 100% free, now and always. It is currently available on TestFlight. Syncing needs an active Droppy license on the Mac you pair with. It is iPhone only, on iOS 26 or later: there is no iPad build and no Apple Watch app.

## Pairing, about a minute

- Open Droppy on your MacIt lives in the menu bar and in the notch.

- Go to Settings, General, Droppy SyncCreate a Sync Library if you have not already.

- Choose Pair iPhoneYour Mac shows a QR code, with the link written out under it.

- Scan it, or paste itConfirm on the phone. Camera is the only permission it asks for, and nothing joins your library without you approving it there.

## How the encryption works

 Your Mac creates the key and keeps it in its Keychain. It reaches your iPhone inside the pairing code you scan, and never reaches a server. Every record is sealed on the device before it is sent, so the server only ever holds what it cannot read. A pairing code works once, then expires.

## What syncs

 Six stores, both directions. Change something on either end and the other catches up on the next pass.

 ClipboardEverything your Mac copied, with the app it came from. Search it, filter to favourites, copy it back.

 File traysThe files in your Mac's file tray and floating file tray. Open one, rename it, or hand it to another app.

 OCREvery text grab, still attached to the image it came from. The phone can add to it from the camera.

 NotesThe same notes your Mac's Notes widget keeps. Write one here and it is in the notch.

 Cloud linksEvery share link you made, from either device, with a copy button and a real delete.

 Your devicesRevoke a phone, rotate its credential, or leave the library. Your license card is here too.

## Managing the library

- Revoke a device from either end. It immediately loses access to the Sync Library. Content it already downloaded stays on it, and pairing it again needs a new code from the Mac.
 
- Reset Sync Library deletes the library from the server and starts a fresh one on this Mac. Nothing on the Mac is deleted and other devices keep what they downloaded, but every device has to be paired again.
 
- If your license lapses, the pairing survives and Droppy says so, instead of quietly unpairing the phone.

## Privacy and data

## Troubleshooting

 The handful of things that come up most, and what fixes them.

 The notch or island does not appear

 Check three switches, in this order. Under Settings, Accessibility, make sure Hold to reveal is off, or hold the modifier combination it is set to. Under Settings, General, Visibility, check Show on external displays, Per-display visibility and Hide on fullscreen. Then confirm Accessibility permission is granted under Settings, Permissions, since without it Droppy cannot place its overlay.

 A shortcut does nothing

 Global shortcuts need Accessibility permission. If that is granted, the combination is probably claimed by something else: open Settings, Shortcuts and re-record it, and Droppy names the conflict. Clipboard item shortcuts are a special case: they only work while the clipboard window is open and focused.

 The visualizer does not move, or per-app volume is missing

 Both read audio levels, so macOS asks for System Audio Recording once. Grant it from the row under Settings, Permissions. Droppy only reads levels in the moment and never records.

 Dragging a file moved it instead of copying it

 That is macOS behavior: a drag to the same disk moves, a drag to another disk copies. Turn on Protect originals under Settings, General, File actions and Droppy always copies instead.

 The menu bar icon is gone

 If you hid it, right-click the notch silhouette where it used to sit to bring it back. If it is only hidden behind other menu bar items, the Thaw Droplet manages menu bar crowding and can keep Droppy visible.

 My iPhone will not pair, or nothing is syncing

 A pairing code works once and then expires, so generate a fresh one from Settings, General, Droppy Sync if you scanned an old screenshot. Syncing needs an active license on the Mac. If Droppy says the library is unavailable, check that a Sync Library exists on this Mac, and remember that a reset library requires every device to pair again.

 A Droplet asks for a runtime or an app I do not have

 A few Droplets depend on something external: Remove Background and Voice Transcribe install a runtime and a model once, Video Compress installs FFmpeg, PDF Compress installs Ghostscript, and Alfred, Kaset and LocalSend need their own app. Each Droplet's page says exactly what it needs before you install it, and the install is one button.

 Droppy will not open, or says it is in the wrong place

 Move Droppy to your Applications folder and launch it from there. Running it from the disk image or from Downloads breaks updating and some permissions, and Droppy tells you so rather than misbehave quietly.

## Thunderstorm

 A full Spotlight replacement

 Thunderstorm is Droppy's own launcher. Press Option-Space, or the shortcut you set on Droppy's Shortcuts page, to search your apps, files and System Settings pages, calculate and convert units, money, dates and time zones, and search the web. Droppy is in it too: its commands, your droplets with their widgets and settings, Droppy's settings, clipboard history and the file tray come up in the same search. Add quicklinks, snippets and commands of your own, move windows and keep workspaces, chat with AI or fix, rewrite and translate the text you select, and give anything it lists an alias, a hotkey or a place in your favorites.

## What it does

- Option-Space by default, or any shortcut you set on Droppy's Shortcuts page
 
- Search apps, files, System Settings pages, clipboard history and Droppy in one panel
 
- Quicklinks, snippets that expand as you type, and shell and script commands
 
- Window commands, saved layouts, workspaces and a window switcher
 
- A calculator for units, currencies, dates and time zones
 
- An alias, a hotkey or a favorite for anything it lists, and links that run it
 
- Quit or uninstall apps, control volume and media, lock, sleep or restart
 
- AI chat and quick actions on this Mac or with your own key, and extensions in Alpha

## How to use it

- Turn it on from the Droplet Store. It opens on Option-Space; record another shortcut on its Open panel row on Droppy's Shortcuts page.
 
- Type to search apps, files, System Settings pages, Droppy's settings and commands, your Droplets, clipboard history and the file tray at once.
 
- Press Return to open the highlighted row, or ⌘K for everything else it can do. Each action in that menu shows its shortcut, and the shortcut works straight from the list.
 
- Choose Expanded or Compact under Window on its page. Expanded lists your favorites and apps under the search bar; Compact is the bar alone, with results once you type.

## Search and ranking

- One search covers apps, files and folders, System Settings pages, Droppy's settings and droplets, system commands, your library of quicklinks, snippets, scripts and notes, extensions, the calculator, the web, clipboard history, the file trays and window commands. Each kind has its own switch under Search results.
 
- A small slip still finds things: one wrong letter in four or more (safri), initials (vsc for Visual Studio Code, gb for GarageBand) and scattered letters in order. Those rank below anything that matches better.
 
- Apps answer to their English name, their names in other languages, older names like iTunes or System Preferences, and Latin spellings of names in other scripts. A bundle identifier such as com.apple.Safari finds its app too.
 
- It learns: what you open for a search moves up the next time you type it. ⌘1 to ⌘0, hotkeys and links never teach it. Reset Ranking in ⌘K forgets one row, and Reset learned ranking in its settings forgets everything.
 
- Type a section's name, such as Quicklinks, Snippets, System Settings, Window management or Extensions, to list the whole section.
 
- With nothing typed, the expanded launcher shows your favorites, then Suggestions (a few things you often open at this time of day), then your apps. The switches under Before you type add system commands, quicklinks, snippets, window commands, layouts, workspaces and extensions below them.
 
- A running app has a small dot under its icon.

## Aliases, favorites and hiding

- Set Alias… in ⌘K gives any row another name, shown as a small tag after its title. Typing the alias puts that row first. Two rows never share an alias: Thunderstorm asks before it moves one.
 
- Type an alias and a space to go straight to the fields of a quicklink, snippet or command that needs input.
 
- ⇧⌘F adds any row to Favorites, System Settings pages and droplet pages included. ⌥⌘↑ and ⌥⌘↓ change their order.
 
- ⇧⌘H hides a row from search, with Undo in the pill. Its favorite, alias and hotkey keep working, and Hidden from search in its settings brings it back.

## The panel and its keys

- ⌘K, or a right-click, opens the actions of a row. The menu has its own search field, and Escape closes only the menu.
 
- Escape clears what you typed, then goes back a screen, then closes. ⌘Esc goes back to the main search from any screen, and Delete on an empty field goes back one. Escape key can make it close and reset at once instead.
 
- A quicklink, snippet or command that needs input shows labelled fields after its name. Tab moves between them, and Return runs once every required field is filled.
 
- With nothing typed, Tab moves on to AI Chat (once AI is on) and Clipboard History, and ⇧Tab goes back. Space opens Search Files.
 
- The compact bar can end with up to five favorites, opened with ⌘1 to ⌘5. ↓ shows the full list.
 
- Drag the launcher by its search bar to place it. It snaps to the middle of the screen and its usual height, remembers the spot per display, and Recenter in its footer menu puts it back.
 
- A pill confirms what an action did: a check when something changed, a dot when there was nothing to do, and a red mark with the reason when it failed.
 
- The keys are listed under Inside Thunderstorm in the keyboard shortcuts reference.

## Apps and system commands

- ⌘K on an app adds Get Info and Share…, and for a running app Hide, Hide Others, Restart Application, Quit Application and Force Quit, which asks first.
 
- Uninstall… lists the app and its leftover files in your Library folders, each with a checkbox and its size. Folders matched only by name start unchecked, and anything that needs Full Disk Access stays where it is. Everything goes to the Trash, and Put Back in the pill undoes it.
 
- System commands: Lock Screen, Sleep, Log Out, Restart, Shut Down, Quit All Apps, Hide All Apps, Eject All Disks, Start Screen Saver, Show Desktop, Toggle Dark Mode, Toggle Stage Manager, Dismiss Notifications, Play or Pause, Next Track, Previous Track, the volume commands, Open Trash, Empty Trash, Toggle Hidden Files, Toggle Bluetooth, Toggle Wi-Fi and Toggle Microphone.
 
- Log Out, Restart, Shut Down, Quit All Apps and Empty Trash ask first in a dialog. Quit All Apps leaves Finder and Droppy open.
 
- Volume commands step by 5 percent and show the level in Droppy's own volume HUD.
 
- System commands under Search results has a switch for every one of them.

## Quicklinks and snippets

- A quicklink opens a website, a search, a file, a folder or an app's link by its name or keyword, in the app you choose. Create Quicklink makes one, and Save as Quicklink… in ⌘K turns a link, a file or a web search into one.
 
- A snippet is text you paste from Thunderstorm, or expand in any app by typing its keyword once Expand keywords as you type is on. That switch is off until you turn it on, and it needs Input Monitoring. Save as Snippet… in ⌘K makes one from clipboard text.
 
- Placeholders fill in when it runs: {argument} (with a name, a default or a list of choices), {selection}, {clipboard}, {date}, {time}, {uuid}, {cursor} and {snippet name="…"}. Modifiers such as | trim or | uppercase change a value, and the editor previews the result.
 
- Search Quicklinks and Search Snippets list everything with a detail pane, to open, copy, edit, duplicate, pin with ⌘. or delete. Both import and export a readable JSON file and skip duplicates.
 
- Snippets folder keeps each snippet as a Markdown file, to sync with iCloud Drive or Git, and picks up edits made outside Droppy. A Markdown snippet pastes as rich text where the app takes it.

## Shell and script commands

- A command can ask before it runs, and show its output in a window with live colors, Stop, Run Again and Copy All. Otherwise its last line shows in a pill.
 
- Every script in the scripts folder becomes a command, set up by its header: title, mode, icon, arguments (text, password or dropdown), confirmation and working folder. An inline script shows its first line of output on its row and can refresh on a schedule.
 
- Import Raycast Scripts copies a folder of script commands into Thunderstorm, icons included.
 
- Your Apple Shortcuts are commands too. Type a shortcut's name and some text to run it with that text as its input.

## Backup
